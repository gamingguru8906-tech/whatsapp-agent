## Calculating the Personal-Year Number

The speaker frames career and business success as a matter of aligning actions with opportunities, while acknowledging that money and material resources are necessary for everyday life. The advice is presented for people willing to work, not for those seeking shortcuts such as winning a lottery or gambling.

To calculate a personal-year number, add the **day of birth**, the **month of birth**, and the digits of the **year being considered**, reducing the result to a single digit. For example, for someone born on August 18, the day and month total 26; adding the reduced value of 2025, which is 9, gives 35, which reduces to 8. To consider a future year, use that year instead—for example, the speaker says 2026 reduces to 1.

| Step | What to add | Example given in the session |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Day of birth and month of birth | August 18: 18 + 8 = 26 |
| 2 | Add the reduced digits of the target year | The speaker uses 2025 → 9 and 2026 → 1 |
| 3 | Reduce the total to one digit | 18 + 8 + 9 = 35 → 8 |

The speaker presents this calculation as a way to identify the “personal year” and then suggests different behaviors for each number. These interpretations and remedies are the speaker’s numerology framework, rather than demonstrated causal mechanisms.

As the flow shows, the target year matters: the same birth date can produce a different personal-year number when the year changes.

## Guidance for Personal Years One and Two

### Personal year one: initiative, visibility, and respect for senior people

The speaker describes year one as a beginning after darkness: progress may feel slow at first, and a person may feel stuck or underappreciated before opportunities become clearer. The suggested response is to build a morning routine and make use of morning sunlight; the speaker also recommends keeping a rising-sun image in the work area.

The guidance links the sun with energy and giving. The speaker therefore recommends feeding people on Sundays—through community meals, a shared meal, or food prepared and given away—and says generosity is important in a year one. For the second half of the year, the speaker advises dressing impressively, arguing that an overly casual or “lazy” presentation could reduce how others perceive someone.

The speaker adds that, within this numerology and astrology framework, a poorly placed Sun could be associated with reputational problems or job loss. As an informal sign of an “unbalanced” Sun, the speaker points to conflict with elders or one’s father, and recommends respecting senior colleagues or business contacts. For a struggling business, the speaker suggests bringing a senior person—such as a retired military officer—into a position in the business.

### Personal year two: routine, support, and care for others

The speaker associates year two with the Moon and the mind, which they characterize as inclined toward comfort and away from discipline. The practical advice is to follow a fixed routine: show up even when motivation is low, go to the office rather than defaulting to working from home, and maintain a proper work station.

The speaker also connects year two with the image of moonlight guiding travelers in darkness. They recommend helping someone experiencing hardship, especially by supporting a child’s education through school fees or books. The rationale offered is that providing help and spreading happiness will return as happiness.

For people managing teams or businesses, the speaker advises eating with more junior colleagues or team members about once a week and adopting a nurturing, “mother-like” approach—listening to people’s concerns and helping meet their needs. The speaker also says year two may be a favorable time for property investment if one can afford it; if not, they suggest investing in silver instead.

## Personal Year Three: Communication, Learning, and Preparation

For year three, the speaker recommends being deliberate about language and beginning the day with “Beauty, Knowledge, and Laughter” (BKL). The suggested routine is to compliment oneself, learn an idea, share it with three people, and start the day cheerfully. The speaker describes year three as a potentially fortunate period for advancement, public recognition, or business growth, while warning that repeatedly complaining about one’s work may reinforce dissatisfaction.

The speaker also recommends studying something new, learning a skill that increases professional value, and maintaining physical fitness. They link these actions to preparation for the following year, which they describe as year four: in their framework, year four reflects the results of what a person did in year three. Neglecting fitness may, in the speaker’s account, be followed by illness; failing to build skills may leave someone feeling behind.

## Personal Year Four: Organization, Relationships, and Financial Caution

The speaker says that people in year four should focus on letting go rather than clinging to things. They recommend strengthening relationships and working bonds with teammates, stakeholders, vendors, and suppliers, partly to reduce the chance of being misunderstood.

The speaker associates year four with Rahu, an astrological concept, and says results may depend on its placement in a birth chart. They mention the first and fifth houses as favorable placements, and the twelfth house, or the Shatabhisha and Swati lunar mansions (nakshatras), as possible sources of difficulty. They advise people to know their birth chart, nakshatra, and Moon sign, and suggest asking an astrologer; they express less trust in generic online reports.

The practical cautions are to avoid taking large loans and to be careful with credit cards, because the speaker portrays Rahu as creating an illusion that money is about to arrive, encouraging spending before it does. To regain a sense of control, the speaker recommends keeping one’s surroundings and records organized: maintain papers, insurance documents, licenses, degrees, emails, and WhatsApp chats so that important information can be found quickly.

The speaker also warns against withdrawing socially during year four. Instead, they advise attending seminars, webinars, meetings, and professional gatherings. They recommend adding extra time to work—for example, starting half an hour early—and claim that this can improve profitability, cash flow, productivity, and control over work.

## Personal Year Five: Move Quickly, but Use Experience and Relationships

The speaker describes year five as a “breath of fresh air” after a difficult period, bringing an urge to do something new. They caution against letting excitement replace foresight: rather than rushing into a partnership, the speaker recommends applying skills and experience gained in earlier years.

Because the speaker calls year five fast-paced, they advise actively expanding one’s network. The suggested self-check is to consider how many people know you and how many people you know; the speaker says that staying within a very small circle may limit results. They encourage meeting new people, introducing oneself confidently, and treating conversations as potential sources of results or referrals.

The speaker’s suggested practices include keeping plants and greenery well maintained and lighting a ghee lamp by a tulsi plant each day. For someone who feels stuck, the speaker recommends putting water in a green glass bottle, leaving it in sunlight for one to two hours, and drinking it daily; they describe this as activating the heart chakra and helping clear personal or professional blockages.

## How the speaker calculates and interprets a personal year

The speaker calculates a personal-year number by adding the birth **day**, birth **month**, and the **current year**—not the birth year—and then reducing the sum to the number being discussed. For example, the speaker gives 1 May 2025 as 1 + 5 + 9 = 15, then 1 + 5 = 6; for 10 June 2025, the sum is 10 + 6 + 9 = 25, then 2 + 5 = 7.

> These numerology and astrology interpretations, predictions, and remedies are presented as the speaker’s beliefs and advice; the excerpt does not establish them as scientifically validated methods.

## Personal year 6: spending, work, relationships, and health

The speaker advises people in a personal year 6 to watch their spending and manage cash flow carefully, because expenses may rise and create financial strain. The recommendation is to avoid unnecessary purchases and, if using installments, favor things that may grow in value or generate income—examples given include gold and a home that can earn rental income—rather than taking on repayments for an under-construction property without considering repayment pressure.

The speaker also suggests paying attention to appearance: updating the wardrobe and discarding clothes that are no longer wanted. In personal life, the year is described as potentially bringing progress such as marriage or having a baby, while the speaker associates a “negative Venus” with reputational or personal difficulties. The speaker’s suggested informal signs of negative Venus include frequent negative thoughts, low confidence in one’s body, unwanted sexual thoughts or fantasies, and spending despite knowing that money is insufficient.

For career, the speaker says growth may come from seeking a change of role or workplace, or asking one’s manager for additional responsibilities. Business owners are advised to emphasize sales, marketing, and visibility. The speaker also urges people to focus on health, food, and exercise, warning that neglect could make the following two years more difficult.

The speaker says people in a 6 year may feel that others are jealous of them and recommends a black obsidian bracelet as protection from surrounding negativity: men are told to wear it on the right hand and women on the left.

## Personal year 7: learning, confidence, communication, and routines

The speaker describes personal year 7 as a period for introspection and new learning. The advice is to start learning something early in the year, because otherwise a person may feel unprepared for what lies ahead; the speaker also says confidence can fall during this period.

To address low confidence and energy, the speaker recommends building a habit of going to the gym, specifically emphasizing showing up regularly. The speaker also suggests bathing with a pinch of turmeric and drinking water in which a piece of cinnamon has soaked overnight in a silver cup, presenting these as remedies for allergies, frequent illness, or low energy even when a doctor says things are fine.

For clearer thinking and fewer misunderstandings, the speaker advises writing down plans and instructions instead of relying on verbal communication, and meeting people in person rather than discussing important matters by phone. The speaker also recommends drawing the Ketu yantra and Mercury yantra in a notebook every day, describing these as a first-aid measure for people who feel stuck or unable to make progress.

The speaker says a personal year 7 may be especially difficult for people whose name number is 4 or 8, and advises keeping the kitchen clean and organized and removing expired food. The speaker presents these small household practices as potentially consequential for the year.

The speaker recommends a black rutile quartz bracelet—left hand for women and right hand for men—as a way to avoid negativity. If a bracelet is not possible, the alternative offered is to carry five cloves in a small cloth pouch and replace them every Friday, putting the old cloves in soil.

The speaker describes a year 8 as favorable for buying land, but advises against borrowing money, pledging gold, or taking on debt; instead, the speaker suggests selling land if funds are needed and buying again in a later year.

The speaker also links progress in a year 8 with leg strength and recommends exercises such as squats and lunges. In a personal response, the speaker advises a person who feels stuck to check their name number, attend to physical fitness, and consider possible hormonal, skin, or hair-fall concerns; the speaker additionally suggests widening one’s social circle, attending a spiritual organization, learning Sudarshan Kriya, or using a seven-minute morning meditation, as well as wearing more white, blue, and pink and a tiger’s-eye bracelet on the left hand.
